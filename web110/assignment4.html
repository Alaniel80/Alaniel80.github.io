According to PowerDMARK, a bulk email sender is any individual or organization that sends 5,000+ messages per day from the same domain. Typically includes automated notifications, promotions, newsletters, and transactional messages sent through marketing platforms or CRMs. Recipients may or may not have interacted with the sender. Bulk email senders must follow strict rules to avoid spam and phishing. For example, for Google, key requirements will be: • Threshold: 5,000/day • SPF, DKIM, DMARC implementation • One-click unsubscribe (RFC 8058 headers) • Enforces ARC for forwarded mail • Uses permanent 550 errors for non-compliance • Full enforcement since 2025 Source: PowerDMARC. I use Gmail in my everyday life and Outlook at my workplace, so instead of simply listing the steps for sending and replying to email messages, I decided to compare the two platforms. This comparison helps me to understand why large organizations tend to prefer Outlook over Google’s email service. Platform: Gmail - Runs in any browser, no installation; Outlook - Required Part of Microsoft 365, installed software Interface: Gmail - Uses labels and tabs, easy to use, theme customization, light/dark mode; Outlook - Uses folders and panes, custom color schemes, built-in themes. Integration: Gmail - Google Drive, Sheets, Docs, Meet; Outlook - Microsoft Office, Teams, SharePoint. AI features: Gmail - Built-in Gemini AI (mostly free); Outlook - Built-in Copilot (requires paid license for cross-app intelligence). Offline use: Gmail - Limited through browser settings; Outlook - Full offline access to emails, calendar, and contacts. Security: Gmail - Google security, 2-step verification, phishing protection, encrypted connections; Outlook - Enterprise-grade security, advanced thread protection, data loss prevention, encrypted connections. Overall, Gmail is web-based and easy to use for personal communication, with access from any browser. Outlook, however, provides stronger security features and deeper integration with Microsoft 365 applications. These capabilities are essential for businesses that require centralized management, shared calendars, compliance controls, and secure communication.
